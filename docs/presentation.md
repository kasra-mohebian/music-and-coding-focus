# Does Music Help You Code? — three-slide presentation

Original public presentation outline based on this review. Source IDs resolve through the [references](../REFERENCES.md).

## Slide 1 — A practical research question

**When music feels helpful, does work performance improve too?**

Compare three outcomes: perceived focus, measured task performance and programming-specific results. Use a rapid-review protocol to classify the 18 bibliography records before drawing conclusions.

Speaker note: Developer habits motivate the question. Feeling productive and producing more correct code require different evidence.

## Slide 2 — What the studies actually show

- Developer survey: perceived benefits and work context (M01).
- Tiny programming EEG pilots: inconclusive music results (M04).
- Attention task: reported focus improves without a significant mean reaction-time gain (M11).
- Cognitive tempo/valence comparison: no silence baseline (M13).

Speaker note: Separate direct, mixed and indirect tasks. M11/M12 share a dataset; M10 extends M09. Six references still need full-text access for deeper appraisal.

## Slide 3 — The defensible conclusion

**Music choices depend on the task, person and environment.**

Compare perceived focus with actual outcomes when evaluating your own working routine. The reviewed evidence does not establish a universal playlist, volume, tempo or coding-performance guarantee.

Speaker note: The contribution is a source-linked evidence trail and a precise statement of what remains uncertain. Show the study register and appraisals so the audience can inspect the reasoning.
